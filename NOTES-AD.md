
## Transaction detail

The still-life is the best drawing of the four. Table ellipse, cream
speckle, bottle, and the cobalt shadow pool with its stippled edge all
land. Four things to fix.

1. **The amount is blurred.** `−₹2,850` carries a soft dark-red offset
   behind it that reads as a blur or a double-exposure, not as riso
   misregistration. Misregistration in print is a *crisp* second
   impression offset by a hair — a shadow is just a shadow. Either make
   it a hard 1px offset in a second ink or remove it. As drawn it looks
   like a rendering fault.
2. **The note truncates mid-word** — "Split three ways, I c…". Let it
   wrap to two lines, or shorten the row's content.
3. **The chair fights the table.** The blue table pedestal shows straight
   through the chair's back opening, so the two objects read as one flat
   tangle. In the comp the chair sits cleanly in front and occludes the
   pedestal.
4. The seat's crosshatch is too fine and goes muddy at this size. Coarsen
   it or drop it for a flat fill with grain.

Minor: a large empty green gap sits between the date and the table. The
comp fills that space; ours reads as a layout that ran out of content.

## Goal detail

The temple is genuinely good — the columns are crisp and evenly rhythmic,
which is exactly where this drawing could have failed. The land under it
is the problem.

1. **The headland is a featureless slab.** It fills the bottom ~45% of
   the screen as one flat green mass with no profile. The comp builds its
   headland from layered silhouettes with a strong diagonal falling to the
   sea and visible cliff faces catching light. Ours has no silhouette at
   all — it is a green rectangle with a curved top.
2. **Vertical banding artifacts** stripe the green mass. Whatever is
   producing them (a repeated gradient or a tiling seam) has to go.
3. **The temple has no cliff beneath it.** In the comp the building
   stands high above the water on a drop; ours sits on a lawn that
   continues flat to the bottom edge, which kills the altitude.
4. The sea's horizontal dashes read as placeholder marks rather than
   water. Either commit to a stipple shoreline like the comp or drop them.

Minor: ~180px of empty cream between the meta line and the illustration.
