## Etymological notes

- **adhwë** 'bad': farmers slang (metaphor) from 'stony', Old Basin period. Stony rim soil is the worst land a lineage can be given.
- **adhwë** 'stony, barren': Proto-Basin derivative: \*hatu 'stone' + \*-ka
- **adhyol** 'stranger, outsider': youth slang (compound) from 'stone' + 'mouth', Middle Basin period. 'Stone-mouth': pejorative for rim and Highland people.
- **athlan** 'poor rim land': Modern Basin 'stone' + 'field'
- **barlëm** 'boatman': Proto-Basin derivative: \*baru 'boat' + \*lama 'person'
- **bedhvë** 'granary': Proto-Basin derivative: \*beti 'grain' + \*bin 'house'
- **bedibin** 'treasury (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin bedibin; doublet of inherited 'granary'
- **beth** 'wealth': market slang (metonymy) from 'grain', Old Basin period. Grain was the market unit of account.
- **bethko** 'trade route': Modern Basin 'grain' + 'road'
- **beyu** 'number, arithmetic (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin beju; doublet of inherited 'count'
- **bii** 'eat (herders register only)': herders slang (metaphor) from 'graze', Middle Basin period. People 'graze' like cattle; mock-rustic, later neutral.
- **chit** 'perish (of animals)': Proto-Basin root; meant 'die' until displaced by farmers slang
- **dalgë** 'head': herders slang (metaphor) from 'gourd', Old Basin period. Drovers' joke: a skull is a dried gourd (cf. Latin testa 'pot' → French tête).
- **doslënrë** 'the Basin people (endonym)': Proto-Basin derivative: \*dos 'bowl' + \*lama 'person' + \*-ra
- **duntër** 'herd': Proto-Basin derivative: \*dunto 'cattle' + \*-ra
- **döspëvnë** 'the Basin language (endonym)': Proto-Basin derivative: \*dos 'bowl' + \*kʷibe 'say' + \*-ni
- **edhër** 'farmer': Proto-Basin derivative: \*ete 'till' + \*-ter
- **gal** 'die': farmers slang (euphemism) from 'fallow', Old Basin period. Death taboo: 'he lies fallow' = he rests and will return in the cycle.
- **ganu** 'council member (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin ganu; doublet of inherited 'elder'
- **gon** 'lawful, proper': Proto-Basin root; meant 'good' until displaced by youth slang
- **ka** 'good': youth slang (metaphor) from 'fat', Middle Basin period. In a surplus economy fatness signals prosperity.
- **kandëbün** 'fresh flood deposit': Modern Basin 'new' + 'silt'
- **kandëlou** 'first-sheaf festival': Modern Basin 'new' + 'sheaf'
- **katsuk** 'toll, pass tax': Highland qʼatsuk, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **kerlë** 'lamb': Proto-Basin derivative: \*kar 'sheep' + \*-li
- **khërek** 'goat': Highland χrek, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **kiprë** 'felt': Highland kipr, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **kospi** 'statute (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin kospi; doublet of inherited 'law'
- **koth** 'on, atop': Middle Basin 'head' koð, reduced
- **koth** 'summit, top of a mountain': Proto-Basin root; meant 'head' until displaced by herders slang
- **kë** 'will (future proclitic)': Middle Basin 'go' koð, reduced
- **këkiv** 'Highlander (exonym)': Highland kqiw 'people', borrowed into Middle Basin
- **lef** 'from (source)': Middle Basin 'spring' lev, reduced
- **ley** 'not (postverbal)': Middle Basin 'seed' lej, reduced
- **liy** 'knife': Highland lij, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **ludhël** 'eight': Proto-Basin derivative: \*lu 'five' + \*tel 'three'
- **lumwë** 'seven': Proto-Basin derivative: \*lu 'five' + \*nuko 'two'
- **lwazët** 'nine': Proto-Basin derivative: \*lu 'five' + \*hasot 'four'
- **lwim** 'six': Proto-Basin derivative: \*lu 'five' + \*himo 'one'
- **mavëk** 'go (boatmen register only)': boatmen slang (metaphor) from 'drift', Middle Basin period. Boatmen 'drift' everywhere.
- **mel** 'behind, after': Middle Basin 'back' melkə, reduced
- **mon** 'know (market register only)': market slang (metaphor) from 'grasp', Middle Basin period. 'Grasp' a price = understand it.
- **mul** 'foreign guest under hospitality law': Proto-Basin root; meant 'stranger, outsider' until displaced by youth slang
- **mun** 'inherited wealth in cattle (legal term)': Proto-Basin root; meant 'wealth' until displaced by market slang
- **nakå** 'when': Middle Basin 'what' na + kaː: 'what' + 'day': on what day
- **napakh** 'iron': Highland napaχ, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **natel** 'why': Middle Basin 'what' na + tel: 'what' + 'root': cause pictured as the root of a plant
- **natu** 'where': Middle Basin 'what' na + tu: 'what' + 'in' (< 'belly'): in what place
- **navil** 'how': Middle Basin 'what' na + wil: 'what' + 'with' (< 'hand'): by what means
- **neschë** 'young (of animals)': Proto-Basin root; meant 'small' until displaced by market slang
- **nor** 'child': farmers slang (metaphor) from 'sprout', Old Basin period. Children are a household's new growth.
- **nothko** 'toward the central marsh (inward, downstream)': Middle Basin 'marsh' + '-ward' (< 'road')
- **nou** 'house (farmers register only)': farmers slang (metonymy) from 'hearth', Old Basin period. Part-for-whole: the household is counted by hearths for granary levies.
- **nour** 'lineage, extended household': Proto-Basin derivative: \*nuh 'hearth' + \*-ra
- **nuvlën** 'twenty': Proto-Basin derivative: \*nuko 'two' + \*luno 'ten'
- **në** 'question particle': Middle Basin 'what' na, reduced
- **nëkus** 'cheese': Highland nkʼus, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **nürëk** 'salt': Highland nuriχ, borrowed into Old Basin
- **os** 'before, in front of': Middle Basin 'face' os, reduced
- **ovlën** 'hundred': Proto-Basin derivative: \*hobu 'big' + \*luno 'ten'
- **pas** 'very (intensifier)': boatmen slang (metaphor) from 'flood', Middle Basin period. 'Flooding' = in great quantity; boatmen's intensifier.
- **pibeni** 'grammar, formal language (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin pibeni; doublet of inherited 'speech'
- **pit** 'say (market register only)': market slang (metaphor) from 'thresh', Middle Basin period. Market chatter as 'threshing': beating out words.
- **pivnë** 'speech, language': Proto-Basin derivative: \*kʷibe 'say' + \*-ni
- **pë** 'the (enclitic)': Middle Basin 'that' pas, reduced
- **ras** 'copper': Highland raʃam, borrowed into Old Basin
- **rup** 'cliff': Highland rpu, borrowed into Old Basin
- **salko** 'toward the mountains (outward, upstream)': Middle Basin 'mountain' + '-ward' (< 'road')
- **salpaf** 'cold downslope wind': Modern Basin 'mountain' + 'wind'
- **savdhër** 'trader': Proto-Basin derivative: \*sawe 'trade' + \*-ter
- **se** 'essence, core (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin seː; doublet of inherited 'heart'
- **sel** 'beside': Middle Basin 'flank' sel, reduced
- **senyë** 'horse-like': Proto-Basin derivative: \*semi 'horse' + \*-ka
- **senyë** 'quick': youth slang (metaphor) from 'horsy', Middle Basin period. Horse-like = fast.
- **silmë** 'friend': herders slang (compound) from 'yoke' + 'person', Old Basin period. 'Yoke-mate': two oxen that pull together.
- **silës** 'eagle': Highland ʃilis, borrowed into Old Basin
- **sokët** 'mountain pass': Highland tsʼoqutʼ, borrowed into Old Basin
- **souvzhith** 'snowmelt': Modern Basin 'thaw' + 'water'
- **tartuzël** 'fertile black soil': Modern Basin 'black' + 'earth'
- **tav** 'kill (guards register only)': guards slang (metaphor) from 'reap', Middle Basin period. Retainers' euphemism-dysphemism: cutting men down like grain.
- **tavani** 'fiscal year, levy period (learned)': re-borrowed by scribes from written Old Basin tawani; doublet of inherited 'harvest'
- **tavnë** 'harvest': Proto-Basin derivative: \*tawa 'reap' + \*-ni
- **tavnë** 'year (farmers register only)': farmers slang (metonymy) from 'harvest', Old Basin period. Years are counted in harvests.
- **te** 'under, below': Middle Basin 'foot' tezəl, reduced
- **tengko** 'clockwise around the basin': Middle Basin 'sun' + '-ward' (< 'road')
- **teyëltër** 'herder, drover': Proto-Basin derivative: \*tajol 'drive' + \*-ter
- **tis** 'wool': Highland tʼiʃ, borrowed into Old Basin
- **tishmë** 'bridle': Highland tiʃm, borrowed into Middle Basin

- **tërun** 'glacier': Highland trun, borrowed into Middle Basin
- **uth** 'small': market slang (metaphor) from 'millet', Old Basin period. Millet is the smallest grain sold.
- **veko** 'counter-clockwise around the basin': Middle Basin 'night' + '-ward' (< 'road')
- **vet** 'pass-warden (official title)': Proto-Basin root; meant 'guard, watchman' until displaced by market slang
- **vil** 'five (market register only)': market slang (metonymy) from 'hand', Old Basin period. Finger counting at grain stalls.
- **vil** 'with (instrument, companion)': Middle Basin 'hand' wil, reduced
- **yol** 'to, at (recipient, goal)': Middle Basin 'mouth' jol, reduced
